Canary sancocho: the salted fish dish eaten with mojo and gofio

When exploring the culinary traditions of the Canary Islands, one dish that stands out is the hearty and flavourful Canary sancocho. This traditional Canary food, known for its use of salted fish, is a staple in island cuisine and a beloved comfort food for locals and visitors alike. The dish is a testament to the islands' rich maritime heritage and their resourceful culinary traditions.

The Canary sancocho combines salted fish, often cherne or burro, with a variety of accompaniments such as papas, batata, pella de gofio, and the ever-essential mojo sauce. Each component of this dish contributes to its unique taste and texture, making it a delightful experience for the palate. Whether enjoyed during family gatherings or festive occasions, sancocho is a dish that embodies the warmth and hospitality of the Canary Islands.

The Essence of Canary Sancocho

What is Canary Sancocho?

Canary sancocho is a traditional dish that epitomizes the rustic and hearty flavours of the Canary Islands. The dish primarily features salted fish, which is desalted and then cooked alongside root vegetables like potatoes and sweet potatoes (batata). What makes this dish unique is the combination of these ingredients with pella de gofio, a dough-like mixture made from roasted grains, and a generous drizzle of mojo sauce, which adds a zesty kick.

Typical Fish Used: Cherne and Burro

The fish used in sancocho is typically cherne or burro, both of which are popular choices in Canary Island cuisine. Cherne, known as wreckfish in English, is prized for its firm texture and delicate flavour. Burro, or white seabream, offers a milder taste that complements the bold accompaniments of the dish. The fish is salted as a preservation method and imparts a distinctive flavour to the sancocho.

The Art of Desalting

Desalting the fish is a crucial step in preparing a traditional Canary sancocho. This process involves soaking the salted fish in water, changing the water several times over a period of 24 to 48 hours. This careful preparation ensures the fish retains its firmness and flavour without being overly salty. It's a delicate balance that requires patience but results in a perfectly seasoned dish.

Accompaniments: More Than Just a Side

Papas and Batata

No sancocho is complete without its traditional sidekicks: papas and batata. Papas, or potatoes, are often served as "papas arrugadas" or wrinkled potatoes, boiled in salt water to achieve their characteristic texture. Batata, or sweet potatoes, adds a hint of sweetness that contrasts beautifully with the salty fish.

Pella de Gofio and Mojo Sauce

Pella de gofio is a staple in Canary cuisine, made by mixing gofio (a type of flour made from roasted grains) with water until it forms a dough. This versatile accompaniment can be shaped into balls or flattened into patties. Meanwhile, mojo sauce, available in red or green variations, is a vibrant blend of spices, herbs, garlic, and vinegar. It is essential for adding a burst of flavour to the sancocho.

When to Enjoy Canary Sancocho

Sancocho is traditionally enjoyed during family gatherings and festive occasions, such as Easter and local festivals. Its communal nature makes it a perfect dish for sharing, bringing people together over a hearty meal. However, its comforting flavours make it a popular choice year-round, providing a taste of home for many islanders.
