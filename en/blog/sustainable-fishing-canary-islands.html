Sustainable fishing in the Canary Islands: how to know your fish is responsible

As the demand for seafood continues to grow, the importance of sustainable fishing in the Canary Islands becomes increasingly apparent. For those dining in Tenerife, responsible fish consumption is not just a choice, but a necessity to ensure the longevity of our oceans and the species that inhabit them. Understanding the nuances of sustainable seafood can enhance your dining experience, whether you're enjoying a meal at a local restaurant or cooking at home.

The Canary Islands, with their rich marine biodiversity, are uniquely positioned to lead the charge in sustainable fishing practices. By choosing seafood that's responsibly sourced, you contribute to the health of marine ecosystems and support local fishermen who adhere to ethical practices. Here's how you can ensure your seafood choices are as responsible as they are delicious.

Understanding Sustainable Fishing in the Canary Islands

Sustainable fishing in the Canary Islands revolves around practices that maintain fish populations at healthy levels and preserve the marine environment. This involves respecting fishing seasons, protecting overfished species, and encouraging the consumption of local seafood alternatives. By doing so, the islands safeguard their waters for future generations while offering quality seafood to locals and tourists alike.

Key Indicators of Sustainability: Labels and Certifications

One of the simplest ways to identify responsibly sourced seafood is through labels and certifications. Look for Marine Stewardship Council (MSC) or Aquaculture Stewardship Council (ASC) labels, both of which signify adherence to sustainable practices. These certifications ensure that seafood is harvested in a manner that minimizes environmental impact, supports fishery management, and upholds social responsibility.

Recognizing Fishing Seasons and Closed Periods

Another critical aspect of sustainable seafood in Tenerife is understanding fishing seasons and closed periods, known locally as "vedas." These are times when fishing for certain species is restricted to allow fish populations to replenish. Choosing seafood that is in season ensures that your meal is part of a responsible consumption cycle.

For instance, at Sal Negra, our menu reflects this commitment. Our "Pescado del día" (fish of the day), available at market price, is sourced based on seasonal availability. This ensures that what you enjoy is both fresh and responsibly caught, contributing to the preservation of marine biodiversity.

Overfished Species and Local Alternatives

With overfishing posing a significant threat to global fish stocks, it's crucial to be aware of which species are most at risk. While certain fish like bluefin tuna and some shark species are overfished, the Canary Islands offer a variety of sustainable alternatives. Opting for local species such as the Atlantic mackerel or the island's beloved vieja (parrotfish) can make a significant difference.

Local Delights: Embracing Canary Island Seafood

The focus on local alternatives is evident in Sal Negra’s offerings. Dishes like the "Pulpo" (octopus), available grilled, fried, or Canarian style for €24.00, showcase the rich flavors of the region while supporting sustainable fishing practices. By choosing local seafood, diners contribute to the preservation of the ocean's delicate balance and enjoy the unique tastes of the Canary Islands.

Supporting Local Fishermen and Markets

Incorporating sustainable seafood practices also means supporting local fishermen who adhere to ethical and environmentally friendly fishing methods. Purchasing directly from local markets not only ensures fresher seafood but also boosts the local economy and promotes traditional fishing practices that have been passed down for generations.

In Costa Adeje and nearby areas like Los Cristianos and Playa de las Américas, numerous markets and restaurants prioritize responsibly sourced seafood. Dining at establishments committed to sustainability, like Sal Negra, ensures that your culinary choices align with efforts to protect the ocean's resources.

Conclusion

Sustainable fishing in the Canary Islands is more than a trend—it's a commitment to future generations. By choosing sustainable seafood, you play an active role in preserving marine life and supporting local communities. Whether you're enjoying a "Parrillada Pescado" (grilled fish platter) at Sal Negra or selecting fresh catch at a local market, every choice contributes to a healthier ocean.
