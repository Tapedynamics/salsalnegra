Langoustine, prawn and shrimp: learn the differences before ordering

When dining at a seafood restaurant like Sal Negra in Costa Adeje, it's easy to be captivated by the array of shellfish on offer. However, distinguishing between langoustine, prawn, and shrimp can be quite a challenge for many diners. Understanding the subtle differences in their appearance, taste, and culinary uses can help you make informed choices when ordering your next seafood dish.

Langoustine vs. Prawn vs. Shrimp: Morphological Differences

At a glance, langoustines, prawns, and shrimp might appear similar, but each has distinct characteristics that set them apart. Recognising these differences can enhance your seafood dining experience and ensure you order exactly what you're craving.

Langoustine

Langoustines, also known as Norway lobsters, are crustaceans that resemble small lobsters. They have a slender body with long, narrow claws and a bright orange-pink hue. Typically found in colder waters, langoustines have a firm yet delicate texture, making them a favourite in Mediterranean cuisine.

Prawn

Prawns are larger than shrimp and have a more pronounced curve in their bodies. Morphologically, they can be distinguished by their branching gills and the fact that their second abdominal segment overlaps the first and third segments. This feature gives prawns a unique appearance compared to shrimp.

Shrimp

Shrimp are generally smaller than prawns and have a more uniform body shape. They possess lamellar gills and a distinct body segmentation where each segment overlaps with the next in a consistent pattern. Shrimp are versatile and found in various cuisines worldwide, including the vibrant seafood dishes at Playa de las Américas.

Differences in Taste and Texture

Beyond their appearance, the taste and texture of langoustines, prawns, and shrimp offer different culinary experiences. Understanding these distinctions will help you appreciate the nuances of each seafood type.

  • Langoustine: Known for their sweet and delicate flavour, langoustines have a firm texture that holds up well to grilling and baking. Their taste is often compared to a cross between lobster and shrimp, with a subtle brininess that makes them a standout choice in high-end dishes.
  • Prawn: Prawns have a slightly sweeter flavour than shrimp, with a firm yet tender texture. They are often used in Mediterranean and Asian dishes, providing a robust seafood taste that complements various spices and sauces.
  • Shrimp: With their mild flavour and tender texture, shrimp are incredibly versatile and can absorb the flavours of the dishes they are cooked in. They are perfect for quick sautés, salads, and pasta dishes, making them a staple in many seafood recipes.

How to Order Shellfish: Identifying Them on the Menu

When you find yourself at a seafood haven like Sal Negra in Costa Adeje, knowing how to order shellfish can elevate your dining experience. Here's a simple guide to help you identify and choose the right seafood types from the menu:

  1. Look for Descriptive Names: Menus often provide hints about the dish through its name. Look for specific names like "Norway lobster" for langoustines or "jumbo prawns" to identify the seafood type.
  2. Read the Descriptions: Pay attention to the dish descriptions, which typically mention the preparation method and accompanying flavours. This can help you determine whether you're ordering a prawn, shrimp, or langoustine dish.
  3. Ask Your Server: Don't hesitate to ask your server for clarification. They can offer valuable insights into the dish's composition and flavour profile, ensuring you make an informed choice.
