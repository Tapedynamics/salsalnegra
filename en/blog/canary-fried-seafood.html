Canary fried seafood: chicharros, cazones and the craft of the right batter

Fried seafood is a staple in Canary Island cuisine, loved by locals and visitors alike for its simplicity and flavor. In Tenerife, the art of frying fish is both a tradition and a craft, with chicharros (small mackerels) and cazones (dogfish) being some of the most popular choices. The secret to perfect canary fried fish lies not just in the fish itself, but in the batter and frying technique employed. Understanding the nuances of temperature, type of flour, and the fish selection is essential for capturing that authentic taste of fried seafood Tenerife is renowned for.

As you stroll through the vibrant markets of Costa Adeje or Los Cristianos, fresh seafood beckons with its promise of crispy, golden perfection. Yet, achieving this at home or even in professional kitchens requires an understanding of the culinary techniques that define Canary fried fish. From selecting the right fish to avoiding common pitfalls in batter preparation, let's delve into the craft of creating that perfect, crunchy delight.

The Art of Choosing the Right Fish

The foundation of excellent Canary fried fish begins with selecting the freshest catch. In Tenerife, chicharros and cazones are go-to options due to their texture and flavor. Chicharros, with their firm flesh, stand up well to frying, while cazones offer a milder taste that complements the batter's seasoning.

Chicharros: The Canary Favourite

Chicharros are small, oily fish that are abundant in the Canary Islands. Their high-fat content makes them ideal for frying as they remain moist inside while achieving a crispy exterior. When selecting chicharros, look for bright eyes and a firm body, indicators of freshness that are crucial for the best frying results.

Cazones: A Mild Alternative

Cazones, or dogfish, provide a soft, white flesh that absorbs the flavors of the batter well. They are less oily than chicharros, making them a lighter option for those who prefer a subtle seafood taste. When preparing cazones, ensure the skin is properly removed before battering to avoid a rubbery texture.

The Craft of the Right Batter

The batter is where the magic happens in Canary fried fish. A well-prepared batter ensures the fish is encased in a light, crisp shell that enhances its natural flavors. The choice of flour, consistency, and frying temperature are all pivotal in this process.

Flour and Consistency

For a classic canary batter fish, a mixture of all-purpose flour and cornmeal can provide the desired texture. The flour gives structure, while cornmeal adds crunch. The batter should be thick enough to coat the fish evenly but not so dense that it becomes heavy. A good rule of thumb is to aim for a consistency similar to pancake batter.

Frying Temperature

Maintaining the correct oil temperature is crucial for a successful fry. Ideally, the oil should be heated to around 180°C (356°F). This temperature ensures that the batter crisps up quickly without the fish absorbing too much oil. An oil thermometer can be a useful tool to avoid fluctuations that lead to soggy results.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Even seasoned chefs can fall prey to common pitfalls when frying seafood. Here are a few tips to ensure your Canary fried fish is always perfect:

  • Overcrowding the Pan: Frying too many pieces at once lowers the oil temperature, resulting in greasy fish. Fry in small batches to maintain temperature.
  • Inadequate Draining: Once fried, place the fish on a wire rack instead of paper towels to prevent the bottom from becoming soggy.
  • Skipping the Rest Period: Allow the batter to rest for a few minutes before frying. This helps the flour hydrate, ensuring a smoother and more even coating.
